# Design review checklist (25 items)

Loaded at every tier: skimmed at Tier 1 (a verdict per item), in full at Tier 2 and Tier 3. Each item that fails becomes a finding of the walkthrough.

- [SR-RVD-026] Give each item a verdict, Solid, Concern, Missing, or Not applicable, with one to three sentences of reasoning that cite a section heading or a line of `design.md`; a Not applicable carries its one-line reason.

## Items

- [SR-RVD-027] Problem framing: the problem lives in the approved requirements, so check that every design element traces to requirement IDs (the `## Requirement traceability` table), that the outcome served is named rather than only the technical activity, and that non-goals match `## Out of scope`; do not re-litigate the requirements here.
- [SR-RVD-028] Stakeholders and ownership: the affected users, the owning team, and the operating team are identified; who answers when it breaks at night is named; upstream and downstream teams are informed; one accountable decision-maker exists.
- [SR-RVD-029] Assumptions and constraints: assumptions are stated rather than buried; regulatory, budget, headcount, deadline, skill, and existing-stack constraints are acknowledged; expected load, data volume, and growth are quantified for day one and a year out; organisational constraints (other roadmaps, vendor contracts, freeze windows) are acknowledged; each assumption is falsifiable.
- [SR-RVD-030] Alternatives considered: two or three alternatives with explicit trade-offs; "do nothing" or "extend the existing system" evaluated; buy versus build considered for any non-trivial component; rejection reasons substantive, not strawmen; no sign of resume-driven, not-invented-here, sunk-cost, or preferred-stack bias.
- [SR-RVD-031] Architecture and component design: clear boundaries with single responsibilities; sound coupling and cohesion with a clear dependency direction; the blast radius of each component bounded; diagrams with legends whose boxes and arrows match the prose; the layers explicit where they matter.
- [SR-RVD-032] Data model and lifecycle: the schema specified with types, nullability, and invariants; one unambiguous source of truth per entity; retention, archival, deletion, and backup and restore addressed; data ownership clear where several services touch the same data; schema evolution addressed; lineage traceable from origin to consumption.
- [SR-RVD-033] Interfaces and contracts: request and response shapes, status codes, and error formats specified; event schemas defined where messaging is used; the versioning strategy explicit; backward and forward compatibility addressed; pagination, filtering, sorting, and rate limits consistent with the rest of the stack; client libraries and command-line surfaces counted as contract surface.
- [SR-RVD-034] Concurrency, consistency, and semantics: transactional boundaries explicit; the consistency model named, not assumed; idempotency, retry safety, ordering, and delivery semantics addressed where they matter; races and concurrent updates anticipated with a resolution; clocks, timestamps, and time zones handled explicitly.
- [SR-RVD-035] Scalability and performance: latency budgets per operation (p50, p95, p99); throughput and capacity targets quantified; the design stress-tested in thought at ten times the load, with what breaks first; bottlenecks identified with the plan for when they are hit; hot keys, skew, fan-out, and unbounded growth considered for data systems; caching (where, which TTL, invalidation) addressed where relevant.
- [SR-RVD-036] Failure modes and resilience: for each external dependency, what happens when it is slow, down, returns bad data, or fails partially; circuit breakers, timeouts, and retries with backoff and jitter; graceful degradation rather than a cascade; a rollback or recovery plan including data state; recovery point and recovery time objectives for stateful components; partial failures (split brain, partial writes) addressed.
- [SR-RVD-037] Observability: service-level indicators and objectives for user-visible operations; logs, metrics, and traces designed in, with correlation identifiers and a sampling strategy; "how do we know it is broken" answered before "how do we fix it"; dashboards and alerts described, not promised; an error-budget policy.
- [SR-RVD-038] Security: a threat model naming attackers, attack surface, and trust boundaries; authentication and authorisation spelled out with least privilege; encryption at rest and in transit with key management and rotation; secrets in a vault or manager, never in plaintext configuration or environment; supply-chain risk considered; tamper-evident audit logging for sensitive actions; injection, request forgery, deserialisation, authentication bypass, and object reference classes considered for the surfaces present; tenant isolation where applicable.
- [SR-RVD-039] Privacy and compliance: sensitive and personal data identified and minimised; data-subject rights (access, deletion, portability, correction) addressed where applicable; the relevant regulatory frameworks named, asked for when unclear; data residency and cross-border movement addressed; consent capture, scope, and revocation handled; segregation of duties for high-impact actions.
- [SR-RVD-040] Cost and operational burden: a rough infrastructure cost for day one and at projected scale; the total cost including development, operations, and lifecycle maintenance; the cost at ten times the scale; on-call rotation, runbook ownership, and operational handoff described; cost attribution possible where it matters.
- [SR-RVD-041] Deployment and topology: multi-region, multi-zone, and multi-tenant considerations; configuration management (what is static, what is runtime, where it lives, who may change it); a deployment strategy (blue-green, canary, rolling, feature flag) fit to the blast radius; development, staging, and production environments described with their data realism; zero-downtime deployment stated as a requirement or not, and achievable if so.
- [SR-RVD-042] Migration and rollout: a step-by-step path from the current state to the target; feature flags, dark launches, shadow traffic, or parallel runs where they fit; a data migration plan with reversibility, idempotency, and progress tracking; user-facing communication where behaviour changes; an explicit backout plan, not "we will roll back"; intermediate states valid and operable.
- [SR-RVD-043] Testability: components testable in isolation with designed-in seams; an integration test strategy (boundaries, fixtures, contract tests); performance and load testing; failure injection for resilience claims; test data sourcing (synthetic, anonymised, fixtures).
- [SR-RVD-044] Timeline and delivery: milestones, estimates, and the critical path belong to `tasks.md`, so check here only the phasing that constrains the design, such as an intermediate state the architecture must support or a dependency between phases the data model must allow.
- [SR-RVD-045] Documentation and runbook: what gets documented, where, by whom, and by when; operator-facing documentation (runbook, troubleshooting, common incidents) part of the deliverable; architectural decisions captured where appropriate.
- [SR-RVD-046] Open questions and risks: an explicit open-questions section (`## Open decisions`); the top risks with mitigations and a decide-by date; an honest "we do not know yet" rather than false confidence; dependencies on unproven technology flagged.
- [SR-RVD-047] Internal consistency: sections proportional in depth to their importance, measured by counting words or bullets per top-level section, taking the median, and flagging any section over three times or under a third of it (the section weight audit of `techniques.md` gives the procedure); terminology consistent throughout; diagrams matching the prose and schemas matching the examples; capacity, latency, and cost numbers reconciled across sections.
- [SR-RVD-048] Overengineering check: for each major component or abstraction, ask what would have to be true for it to be wrong or unnecessary, and flag any that assume load, complexity, or requirements the approved requirements do not justify; in reverse, flag a lightweight choice that projected scale will defeat.
- [SR-RVD-049] Reversibility check: classify each major decision as a one-way door (data models, public APIs, vendor lock-in, persistent storage choices) or a two-way door (internal helpers, deployment scripts); one-way doors get the scrutiny they deserve, two-way doors are not over-debated.
- [SR-RVD-050] Six-months-later test: a new engineer joining in six months could understand from `design.md` alone why these choices were made; flag missing rationale, above all for trade-offs that look arbitrary without context.
- [SR-RVD-051] Design versus reality: always run, since the spec lives in the repository it describes. Spot-check the load-bearing claims against the code (a helper said to exist, a module said to do something, a path said to be the place), without auditing the whole repository, and cite what was read.
